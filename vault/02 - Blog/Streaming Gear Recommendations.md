# Audio
Audio is arguably the most important part of your videos or streams, your video can be laggy and pixelated, but good audio will retain the viewers cause the interaction is mostly verbal.
For YouTube videos, the same applies, bad audio is much more likely to scare your audience away than bad video.

## Techniques

### Controlling Distance
The simplest, most efficient way to improve your audio quality, is to put your pickup (your microphone) closer to the sound source (your voice/your instrument). 
By doing this, you are essentially changing the dynamic between your voice and background audio- the ratio between your voice and background noises will have a higher contrast, and therefore sound better.

### Controlling noise
If you're struggling with blocking out keyboard or mouse noise, it might be worth investing a quieter keyboard & mouse. If you're into mechanical keyboards you can look into tactile or linear switches, or even lube your existing keyboard to eliminate the clicking on your stream.
You can also try finding out the pickup pattern of your microphone, most microphones have a "cardioid" pickup pattern, meaning they pick up the audio from one side of the microphone much better than the other side, orient the diaphragm in a manner that the noise is picked up the least.

### Controlling resonance
Banging your fingers on your keyboard will send waves through the keyboard, through the desk, into your microphone. If your audio is suffering from a lot of droning or resonance, consider getting a microphone boom arm, with a shockmount. this essentially eliminates resonance and other vibrations in the desk. Besides handling noise from the desk, you might be struggling with room resonance as well, try putting a shelf with some books on the wall behind or beside you, this will act as a diffuser, which helps eliminate room reverb.

## Gear

### Microphone
It's important to find a microphone that suits you best, no one microphone is the "end-all-be-all absolute best" microphone. 
Since every voice is different, and every mic treats every voice differently, it's important to experiment or research the type of sound you wish to achieve.
Important in this case is to be conscious about preference bias, research what you have, not what you want. 
If you end up buying something that suits a voice you like, but not the voice you have, you will regret your purchase and might not understand why.

#### Dynamic vs Condenser
If you're loud or have a loud environment, you might benefit from picking a microphone with a greater dynamic range- a dynamic microphone.
If you're relatively consistent in speaking volume, have a treated room, or are recording instruments, the brighter sound of a condenser mic might work better for you.

### Interface
Audio interfaces are devices that manage audio input and output on your computer. If you wish to use XLR microphones, or record instruments, you will need an audio interface to capture the audio from these devices. Most interfaces have microphone inputs, and include microphone pre-amps. the quality of these pre-amps determine how much gain you can get out of your microphone, but bad ones can also produce a pretty high noise floor.
As for outputs, they usually have either 1 or 2 6.35mm jack headphone outputs. If you're using headphones with a 3.5mm jack, you will need [an adapter](https://www.amazon.com/Adapter-6-35mm-Female-Headphone-Connector/dp/B084VGXK45).

In my opinion, aesthetics aren't very important, but if you're building a nice stream set, I can imagine that you wouldn't want something like a [Behringer UMC22](https://www.behringer.com/product.html?modelCode=P0AUX) on your desk.

One of the nicest looking interfaces, I think, is Arturia's [MiniFuse 1](https://www.arturia.com/products/audio/minifuse/minifuse1), which even has a *white* variant, which you rarely see in audio gear. At $99,00 this is also a relatively cheap option. A really attractive option for live streamers that are upgrading from a USB microphone to an XLR microphone.

I'm personally a big fan of Solid State Logic's new [SSL2](https://www.solidstatelogic.com/music-and-audio-production/audio-interfaces) series. with the [SSL2+](https://www.solidstatelogic.com/products/ssl2-plus) you get two separate stereo output channels. This means that if you are using virtual mixing software like Voicemeeter, you could monitor your stream mix quickly using a physical button on the interface.

The [Focusrite Scarlett](https://focusrite.com/en/scarlett) series remains one of the best selling interface series in the world right now. A lot of people (including me) have had some bad experiences with Focusrite's drivers on Windows. When it's working, the DSP mixer is a really nice way to do some low-latency direct monitoring of any input devices you have plugged into the interface. However, this feature isn't present on the cheapest [Solo](https://focusrite.com/en/audio-interface/scarlett/scarlett-solo) and [2i2](https://focusrite.com/en/usb-audio-interface/scarlett/scarlett-2i2) variations.

I've heard conflicting stories about the reliability of [MOTU](https://motu.com/en-us/products/audio-products/) interfaces, but I have no first-hand experiences with these devices, so I would read as many reviews as you can before purchasing any of these.

### Keyboard
Please, please, please don't get clicky switches, they are so ridiculously loud. 
Most viewers won't appreciate the extra click noises.
If you must have a mechanical keyboard (i totally understand), you might look into Gateron yellow switches, very good value, and relatively quiet.
[Keychron](https://keychron.com) offers keyboards with switch options- including Gateron yellows.
